# ── Crop Disease: Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae)
# Source: ICAR – National Rice Research Institute (NRRI)

## Overview
Rice blast, caused by the fungal pathogen Magnaporthe oryzae, is the most devastating disease of rice worldwide. It can cause 10–30% yield losses in India under favorable conditions.

## Symptoms
- Leaf blast: Diamond-shaped lesions with gray centers and brown margins on leaves
- Neck blast: Brown lesion at the base of the panicle (most damaging stage)
- Node blast: Black lesions on nodes causing lodging
- Panicle blast: Brown or black lesions on panicle rachis

## Favorable Conditions
- High humidity (>90%)
- Temperature 25–30°C
- Excess nitrogen fertilization
- Heavy dew and cloudy weather
- Susceptible varieties

## Prevention
1. Use resistant/tolerant varieties (e.g., Pusa Basmati 1509, Swarna Sub1)
2. Avoid excess nitrogen application, especially at tillering
3. Balanced fertilization with potassium and silicon
4. Wider spacing to improve aeration
5. Avoid overhead irrigation if possible
6. Treat seeds with fungicide before sowing (Carbendazim 2g/kg or Trichoderma 4g/kg)
7. Burn/destroy infected crop debris

## Chemical Management
- Tricyclazole 75% WP @ 0.6 g/L water
- Isoprothiolane 40% EC @ 1.5 ml/L water
- Carbendazim 50% WP @ 1 g/L water
- Spray at boot leaf stage and repeat after 15 days if necessary

## Biological Control
- Pseudomonas fluorescens @ 2.5 kg/ha as soil application
- Trichoderma viride @ 4g/kg seed treatment

## Note
Always confirm diagnosis with ICAR-NRRI or your local KVK before applying fungicides. Use only registered pesticides as per CIB&RC guidelines.

# ── Crop Disease: Wheat Rust (Puccinia spp.)
# Source: ICAR – Indian Institute of Wheat and Barley Research (IIWBR)

## Types
1. Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust) – Puccinia striiformis
2. Brown Rust (Leaf Rust) – Puccinia triticina
3. Black Rust (Stem Rust) – Puccinia graminis

## Symptoms
- Yellow rust: Yellow-orange stripes in rows along leaf veins
- Brown rust: Orange-brown pustules scattered on leaves
- Black rust: Large, dark brown/black pustules on stems and leaves

## Favorable Conditions
- Cool, moist weather (10–20°C for yellow rust)
- High humidity and dew
- Dense plant stands

## Prevention
1. Grow rust-resistant wheat varieties (K 307, HD 2781, PBW 343)
2. Timely sowing (November – early December in North India)
3. Avoid late sowing
4. Avoid excess nitrogen

## Chemical Management
- Propiconazole 25% EC @ 0.1% (1 ml/L)
- Mancozeb 75% WP @ 0.25% (2.5 g/L)
- Tebuconazole 25% EC @ 0.1%
- Apply when 5% disease incidence observed; repeat after 15–21 days

## Source
ICAR-IIWBR, Karnal. Contact your state agricultural university for variety recommendations.

# ── Nutrient Management: Nitrogen Deficiency
# Source: TNAU Agritech Portal

## Nitrogen (N) Deficiency Symptoms
- Yellowing (chlorosis) starting from older/lower leaves
- Leaves turn pale green, then yellow, then dry up
- Stunted plant growth
- Reduced tillering in cereals
- Premature leaf drop

## Crops Affected
All crops, particularly cereals (rice, wheat, maize), vegetables, and pulses.

## Causes
- Sandy or low-organic-matter soils
- Waterlogged soils (nitrogen leaching)
- Cool soil temperatures (slow mineralization)
- Excessive rainfall

## Correction
- Apply urea (46% N) @ recommended dose based on soil test
- Rice: 120 kg N/ha (apply in 3 splits)
- Wheat: 120 kg N/ha
- Use fertigation in drip-irrigated crops
- Incorporate organic matter (FYM, compost) to improve N retention

## Caution
Excessive nitrogen application causes:
- Lodging (stem weakness)
- Increased pest and disease susceptibility
- Environmental pollution (nitrate leaching)
Always base fertilizer rates on soil health card recommendations.

# ── Pest Management: Brown Plant Hopper (BPH) in Rice
# Source: Directorate of Rice Research (DRR), ICAR

## Pest
Nilaparvata lugens – Brown Plant Hopper

## Damage Symptoms
- Circular patches of dried, brown plants (hopperburn)
- Yellowing and wilting of plants
- Honeydew secretion on lower plant parts (sooty mold develops)
- Plants fall flat (lodging) in severe cases

## Favorable Conditions
- Dense planting
- Excessive nitrogen
- High humidity and stagnant water

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
1. Use resistant varieties (Jaya, IR 36, IR 64, Swarna)
2. Avoid continuous flooding – drain fields periodically
3. Reduce nitrogen fertilization
4. Light traps to monitor adult populations
5. Release natural enemies: Cyrtorhinus lividipennis (predator)

## Chemical Control (Last resort)
- Buprofezin 25% SC @ 1 ml/L
- Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.3 ml/L (directed at base of plant)
- Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.3 g/L
- Do NOT use broad-spectrum pyrethroids (kill natural enemies)

## Monitoring
- Economic Threshold Level (ETL): 5–10 BPH per hill
- Inspect fields weekly during tillering to heading stage

# ── Irrigation Management in Indian Agriculture
# Source: ICAR – Indian Institute of Water Management (IIWM) + FAO

## Water Requirements by Crop (Approximate)
| Crop       | Water Requirement | Critical Stages               |
|------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Rice       | 1200–1600 mm      | Tillering, flowering, grain filling |
| Wheat      | 450–650 mm        | Crown root initiation, booting, grain filling |
| Cotton     | 700–1200 mm       | Squaring, boll development     |
| Sugarcane  | 1500–2500 mm      | Grand growth period            |
| Groundnut  | 500–700 mm        | Pegging, pod development       |
| Tomato     | 400–600 mm        | Flowering and fruiting         |
| Maize      | 500–800 mm        | Tasseling and grain filling    |

## Efficient Irrigation Methods
1. Drip Irrigation – best for vegetables, cotton, sugarcane
2. Sprinkler Irrigation – suitable for groundnut, wheat, vegetables
3. Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) – rice (saves 25–30% water)
4. Furrow irrigation – for row crops

## Government Schemes
- Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY): Subsidy for micro-irrigation
- Per Drop More Crop: 55% subsidy for small/marginal farmers on drip/sprinkler

# ── Post-Harvest Management
# Source: ICAR – Central Institute of Post-Harvest Engineering and Technology (CIPHET)

## Key Post-Harvest Losses in India
India loses approximately 16–18% of food grains due to improper post-harvest management.

## Best Practices
1. Harvest at proper maturity (moisture content <14% for grains)
2. Use clean, moisture-free storage bags/bins
3. Treat storage structures with malathion or deltamethrin
4. Use hermetic storage bags for small farmers
5. Monitor for weevils, moths, and rodents
6. Cold storage for vegetables and fruits (temperature 0–5°C)

## Supported Schemes
- National Food Security Mission
- Gramin Bhandaran Yojana (rural godown construction subsidy)
Contact your nearest NABARD or state cooperative bank for scheme details.
